For me, an inquiry into bringing Arendt's _The Origins of Totalitarianism_ into conversation with the present day means also engaging with another ambitious manifesto, _The Age of Surveillance Capitalism_ by Shoshanna Zuboff, Harvard Business School professor emerita and the author of _The Age of the Smart Machine. Surveillance Capitalism_ is a lengthy analysis about how Big Data, pioneered by Google, has created a new type of power (instrumental power) that society is subordinated to, beckoning in the “information era” which renders people subject to subliminal control by surveillance capitalists through architectures of behavior modification that are built into modes of modern being through the saturation of daily life with sensor-laden devices. Through countless “uncontracts,” the convoluted terms and conditions through which most “agree” but none read in order to use services funded by surveillance capitalists. These services are essential to many if not most

Zuboff herself cites large chunks of Arendt's _Totalitarianism_ and other texts in _Surveillance Capitalism_, notably summing up her dense chapter on instrumentalism by opposing ‘totalitarianism’ and ‘instrumentarianism’ in an explanatory chart which I have reproduced and annotated in an appendix. It is difficult to reproduce Zuboff’s argument succinctly despite sustained attempts, so I will eschew a through reproduction with the caveat that her argument is lengthy and well-supported with historical and narrative evidence. Her tone is urgent, and while she waxes apocalyptic I believe it’s warranted due to the urgency of these encroaching forces that might be redirected with swift action.

The crux of surveillance capitalism’s claim to power, and a concept essential for an inquiry into conspiratorial trends and censorhip, is what Zuboff calls the _two texts._ The first text is what is visible to us, the users of the connective conveniences of surveillance capitalism, the environment in which the layman navigates the connective technology of the popular internet. The first text is the extremely useful, and cognitively revolutionary services of companies like Google/Alphabet, Facebook, Microsoft, Amazon, and many others: easy access to information via Google search or maps, social interaction via social networks like Facebook. The second text, or the shadow text, is the massive accumulation of data about everything surrounding the first text: how long a user hesitates while scrolling, the speed of typing, a user’s historic location. This all is compounded into a profile which can be used to target people with advertisements or otherwise predict their behavior. 
### Surveillance Capitalism and Arendt's Imperialism:

Zuboff's masterwork puts forth a theory of how the extraction of personal data to fund the endeavors of large tech companies who sell the behavioral change that can be enacted with this data, (like Google, Facebook, and Amazon), is a new iteration of capitalism. Zuboff cites an excerpt from _Origins_, in my edition on page 148, to describe how capitalism develops in waves, each wave marked by a new regime of extraction based on an asymmetric power relationship. However, Zuboff neglects the context in which Arendt invokes this pattern, which is in her discussion of imperialism. I’d like to briefly explore the parallels between imperialism and surveillance capitalism.

I attempted to form an argument that surveillance captialism might be analgous to imperialism, but found that surveillance capitalism is rather enabled by the conditions that have resulted from imperialism, that is, the super-national range of motion which multinational corporations are permitted under the current conditions of the state, primarily in America which is both where surveillance capitalism originated and where I am familiar with the workings of capitalism. (See: [Course - American Capitalism](Course%20-%20American%20Capitalism.md))

Instrumentarian Power:

“Although instrumentarianism and totalitarianism are distinct species, they each yearn toward totality, though in profoundly different ways. Totalitarianism seeks totality as a political condition and relies on violence to clear its path. Instrumentarianism seeks totality as a condition of market dominance, and it relies on its control over the division of learning in society, enabled and enforced by Big Other, to clear its path. The result is the application of instrumentarian power to societal optimization for the sake of market objectives: a utopia of certainty.”(Zuboff 2019, 399)

“Surveillance capitalism is the puppet master that imposes its will through the medium of the ubiquitous digital apparatus. [Zuboff names] the apparatus _Big Other:_ it is the sensate, computational, connected puppet that renders, monitors, computes, and modifies human behavior. Big Other combines these functions of knowing and doing to achieve a pervasive and unprecedented _means of behavioral modification._ Surveillance capitalism’s economic logic is directed through Big Other’s vast capabilities to produce instrumentarian power, replacing the engineering of souls with the engineering of behavior.

Instrumentarian power cultivates an unusual ‘way of knowing’ that combines the ‘formal indifference’ of the neoliberal worldview with the observational perspective of radical behaviorism. […] Thanks to Big Other’s capabilities, instrumentarian power reduces human experience to measurable behavior while remaining steadfastly indifferent to the meaning of that experience” (Zuboff 2019, 376–77). Zuboff coins the useful term _radical indifference_ to describe the moral stance that surveillance capitalists take.

“It is in the nature of instrumentarian power to operate remotely and move in stealth. It does not grow through terror, murder, the suspension of democratic institutions, massacre, or expulsion. Instead, it grows through declaration, self-authorization, rhetorical misdirection, euphemism, and the quiet, audacious backstage moves specifically crafted to elude awareness as it replaces individual freedom with others’ knowledge and replaces society with certainty. It does not confront democracy but rather erodes it from within, eating away at the human capabilities and self-understanding required to sustain a democratic life” (Zuboff 2019, 381)

Zuboff postures herself as something of a successor to Arendt, lauding the beauty of the argues that "surveillance capitalism and its instrumentarian power feed on the volatile 'conditions of existence' that [she summarizes] as the 'collision.' Surveillance capitalism offers solutions to individuals in the form of social connection, access to information, time-saving convenience, and, too often, the illusion of support. [...] more significantly it offers solutions to institutions in the form of omniscience, control, and certainty.

This type of robbery, of a direct connection to our subconcious wants and desires, and of the manipulation of our personality, seems new in its passivity, its invisibility. Under totalitarianism, your will is destroyed through fear and oriented towards the will of one movement, whatever the ends may be (for the regimes Arendt describes, a patriarchal destruction and purification). Under instrumentarianism, it is quietly yoked to many different constraints, and these constraints are different for everyone. There is a silent grooming of the population's subconcious to corporate ends, mainly consumerist and minorly ideological.

My view is that in order to understand the influence of surveillance capitalist companies, we must scrutinize the movements behind the infrastructure that they create, as each conglomerate has a character and motive that drive the forms of their creations. Anyone within the range of behavioral influence of these large corporations with their economic imperatives is under the subliminal influence of their economic, and imperialist-like, motives. By no means to I want to accuse these corporations of being analgous to a totalitarian government, but I see parallels between Arendt's description of movements that led to totalitarian domination through the state apparatus and the movements that are behind the rapid widespread adoption of surveillance capitalist technologies.

### (when I die please find a place to leave my body out for the crows)

I wanted to bury my dog but I didn't know where I could. 

There's no wild place around here, in this suburb where my mom rents. No place wild enough for a dog to decompose undisturbed. No place where the carrion crows are welcome. No place wild enough I need not fear a light at my back as I chip away at icy soil with a shovel. 

I lived on a farm where we could leave bodies out in the open. The animals would make quick work of the remains. Dead bodies were a gift. The microbes longer work of cleaning the bones. Here in the city there is nowhere I know I can do this. 

I wanted to bury my dog but there was no place to go. 

She was a little thing, tall and thin, half poodle. A designer doodle, a princess of a dog. I suppose I'm a princess of a girl too. I wish I could describe her smell to you. But that part doesn't go into words. Allegedly she had bad breath but I didn't mind it. I loved it. It was her. I could smell when she was happy. I can smell when other dogs are happy too, but she was the one who taught me. 

She was old. Sixteen. She never went grey because of how white she was. 

The stranger who came to our house to put her down called her coat -- what, caramel? Some name for her coloring I'd never heard before. *They age so gracefully, those doodles.*

"So, you're a dog death doula? How did you get here?" I made small talk to lighten the mood. But sometimes I hit too heavy when I try that. 

It's an odd thing. When a dog is old and ready to die, we as its masters put it out of its misery. An old, old tale, and a humane one I think. End the pain as a steward. But these days the killing gets contracted out. Normal people in the suburbs don't kill their own dog. 

The Dog Death Doula / Euthanasia Expert, a middle-aged woman with neat pink hair and pawprint tattoos, told me how she had been a vet for over a decade and needed a change of pace. So she started doing mobile euthanasia. 

She took my dead dog away expediently in a polyester blanket. 

Something something Ivan Illitch's theory about professionalization.

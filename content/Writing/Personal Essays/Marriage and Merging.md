### Three Unsuccessful Elopements and a Birth
##### In February, we eloped. Sort of. 

It was a spiritual marriage, with an exchange of rings. We wanted to make a commitment to each other, and the way it worked out was, after finding some rings and blessing them I pulled him up off of one knee and said, "no, this is a mutual thing."

And then we said a bunch of probably beautiful things before asking, "will you be my husband? will you be my wife?" and receiving a Yes. 

That's not what you're supposed to say when you get engaged. 

##### In July we were married again.
I was calling it our "marriage solemnization," because we filled out the papers and told the  state. Witnesses signed and all. My mom flew out, his grandmother flew out. We filmed the vows for posterity. We dressed up in our disheveled sort of way. 

With great grace a friend of ours officiated a quaker-ish wedding. In Quaker weddings the officiant helps the couple commit themselves to each other in front of God, rather than being the direct mediator to God. This jived with our style. We chose a very special location: the Grove of the Patriarchs in the Eastern Sierras in California, the oldest trees in the world.

Once we'd collected Grandma from the airport, we drove up up up to nine thousand feet. We hiked up a couple hundred more and found a spot to lay our blankets in between the gnarled old pines. We asked them to witness us, we asked our family and friends to witness us, we served wine and cheese and grapes from a bag dripping down the vine.  

We said our words and cried. I wore a white dress, barefoot, gnats biting at my ankles. 

Our witnesses signed and dated. We brought the papers to the correct office. A few months later, we got a call from the state of California - our forms had been rejected, and our marriage would remain pending. 

Rejected? Our marriage? 

Papers done incorrectly. Witnesses wrote their addresses slightly outside the lines. You need to re-do the papers. 

Wait, what? By then we had dispersed. The witnesses miles and miles apart. We didn't want to mail papers around. By gosh, we were already married twice!

We respectfully declined.

##### In December we were married again.  
I was nine months pregnant and, anxious about a story I'd read about a baby who wouldn't come out until his parents were properly married, I made us another appointment at the Clatsop County Courthouse. More witnesses, new and old friends. We invited my midwives and they came, one bringing me the gift of a scarf as a veil. 

By then we had decided to change our name. I wouldn't take his, his estranged father's name, and he wouldn't take mine. So we decided to make a new one. We chose Jubilee, a celebration, the forgiveness of debt, a great and ancient biblical cycle. A semi-common English word. 

We filled out the forms with our new name. Uncharacteristically, I misspelled the name of the county we now resided in, but that didn't seem to matter to the state of Oregon. 

Under an archway covered in fake foliage the county clerk pronounced us man and wife. As if we weren't already. We laughed and smiled and kissed. Our friends took pictures and videos. The clerk, happy too, took our paperwork in the back. It was done. 

Wait -- no, it wasn't. The Clerk, flustered, reappeared from the office. 

I'm sorry, you didn't read the fine print. I didn't remember the fine print. You can't do that, change your name to something new. It's illegal. You need to petition separately. It's more time and money. Please cross out what you wrote and write your current names. 

Obediently we took back the papers and crossed out the names we wanted. 

At least we were married. Already, and again, and now officially. 

A few hours later my labor started. 
##### The next morning our son was born. 

It turns out you can name your child anything you want, within certain reasonable parameters. So our son became the first Jubilee. 

### What is a marriage for?
I mean, a big marriage, with the walking down of aisles and dads handing off their daughters and weeping grandmothers. This marriage is for bonding the pair, but it's also about bonding the community. 

Marriage is also about cohabitation. It's about being bonded and committed indefinitely.

many people are married in this way without even knowing it. 

in our world we are conditioned to see marriage as something supreme, irrevocable, something to get to later. Something to get to after a whole life together, after living together, after you are logically sure this is the right one for life. 

But that's also limiting, because it "boyfriend/girlfriend" is a bit demeaning. Be final with it already! Indefinitely is indefinitely! Become together!

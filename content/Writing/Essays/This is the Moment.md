## The Moment

### chiefs, to editors, to anchors, to the algorithm: who curates information?

The invention of the printing press made clear: human connection is intertwined with technology. As the technology we use to communicate changes, so do our "imagined communities." Examples are many: tribal groups using the social technology of storytelling traditions as they did for hundreds of thousands of years; the daily city newspaper or a weekly Sunday Post; nightly national network news; highly targeted endless-scroll 'feed'. 

As traditional news organizations crumble, faced with seismic shifts from competition with advertising-revenue-optimising social media oligopolies, so does our consensus reality. There is no longer a trusted curator of information. Now the algorithm is king.

Reclined in their la-z-boys, Baby Boomers are the last generation to experience a centralized, consensus reality. Fox or MSNBC? Whichever you choose, you are one of millions making sense of the world from the same touchstone. The hierarchy of cable news is neatly stacked. It reaches from coast to coast, every night.

Enter: the feed. With the spread of social media, younger generations have turned away from centralized network media. Now each info stream is personalized. Why wouldn't it be preferable? Each person gets exactly what they want, whether or not they consciously choose it. They get exactly what keeps them there, scrolling.

The algorithmically curated feed has transformed the topology of imagined communities. Rather than 'the nation from coast to coast,' one's media peers reside in an implicit limbo. Your media peers are the ones with the same hidden preferences as you. The feed is modular, specialized, and patchworked. The feed is efficient in an entirely different way than the news hierarchy. Each curator has different priorities. Now, each "filter bubble" has different priorities. 

### Modular organization

What was the last time a technology (like AI) which will increase productivity so much was invented? Electricity!

In the early 20th century, electricity was invented and industrialization [was underway]. Community institutions rose to the challenge of proliferating useful new technologies: Granges oversaw the electrification of America, and the Rotary Club facilitated the transmission of business intelligence across its network. This period was defined by community ownership, with granges and electrical cooperatives owning the electrical infrastructure of their communities.

The rise of scientific management and the post-war social contract [more info or source here?] sidelined the influence of clubs, community organizations, and unions. The topology of the corporate tree came to define human organization. Centralized utility companies bought up electrical infrastructure. People organized their lives around a steady reliable job, forming suburbs and increasing the population of cities. News and consensus reality radiated out from hubs of influence.

In the vacuum left by the post war consensus, there is a growing hunger for new institutions. Network enabled movements that have been incubating sub-surface now have an opportunity to emerge as load-bearing elements of a new America, creating novel forms of community, and new forms of institutions with the potential to shape business and society in ways as monumental as Granges and business clubs of the past.

The electrification of rural America was heralded as a great force of progress, freeing people from hours of daily menial labor. It inspired the construction of the Grand Coulee dam, the largest man made structure at the time of completion. (We need some more examples)

AI has the potential to free us from different kinds of labor too, if shepherded correctly. In order to do so, we will need strong social institutions, like we had before. 

Imagine a new social organization, drawing on the spirit of the electrical cooperative movement. Imagine the creation of compute cooperatives on top. Imagine hundreds of these social organizations, galvanizing people into creative, constructive endeavors, from manufacturing, to construction, to homemaking. Imagine every community in America joining together in this. Imagine what we could build.

# Why it matters

The corporation has been captured by shareholder value incentive, causing cuts to management that holds tacit knowledge and hemorrhaging highly skilled workers. This connects back to scientific management and post-war social contract. Obviously all resulting from globalization. 

For re-industrialization, we must confront a crisis of legacy and tacit knowledge, and an emergence of novel manufacturing techniques powered by robotics and AI. These need to be connected. It's not that the tacit knowledge will be replaced by these advances. Rather the tacit knowledge must be cultivated because advances in manufacturing are multipliers built on top.

Craft is a social and cultural relationship to making. With these emerging topologies of community there is an opportunity to (re)discover the meaning of craft in the context of network technology. To rediscover craft, we need new forms of social infrastructure that can leverage network technology and are up to the task of re-industrializing America.

There is a listlessness in culture, and people need a glue that holds things together. There is potential to foster/discover this. Can feel it happening as part of the vibe shift. Perhaps this is buddho-christian Americana.

This land, the Pacific Northwest, is all I've ever "had." I accepted for a while that I could not claim this land as mine, that it still belonged to the Native peoples from whom it had been stolen, that the right thing to do would be to give the land back. 

From where I stand now, this just won't work. My priority is this land, this Country. This watershed needs stewardship. It's been raped and poisoned and maimed. 

I don't have land to which I'm indigenous. My genes are mixed, I'm a white mutt. And as the Ancestors' guidance have had it, I've learned that no, mine weren't colonizers: my ancestors were some of the first to be colonized. 

When asked about my heritage, I satiate the curious with the myth that Irish blood flows into my freckles. It's a mostly-true story. I've never touched Irish earth. An Irishman told me, *we call your kind Plastic Paddies.* (Then he bought me McDonalds. )

I am American. *Americana do Norte*, North American Woman, a Brazileiro corrects me. I know Irish fairies only from books, visions, imaginations. I know instead the spirits of the Cascades: pines and nettles and cold snow-water over quiet crumbling mountains. The fairies of this forest are trolls and Sasquatch and the shapeshifting elk. And moss too. Lots of moss. I hear these are harmonies with those spirits to be found in Ireland. But I don't know. I've never been there.

My land (or the land that has me) is covered in spiny Himalayan blackberries. Ecologists lament that they are invasive. They are not from here. I grew up pricking myself on their thorns and making their fruits into pies and peering through their thickets to spot bunnies. I grew up watching an unimaginable abundance of their fruits rot, uneaten, by the hghway.

Blackberry fills space made by devastation. Blackberry draws blood from humankind who have ripped from this land its cultures. Who have felled its forests, poisoned its rivers, killed and exiled most of the creatures who lived here before. 

A common story in this age. #Anthropocene. 

Blackberry feeds what's left, protects the earth. The blackberries are prolific. In late summer Blackberries stink up sterile cul-de-sacs with their sweet sticky smells. The rats feast. 

Blackberry says, *this land is in pain. I am its teeth.* Blackberry says, *tread lightly for my abundance.* Blackberry is the brambles around the castle put to sleep for a hundred years. 

The blackberries are not from here but like me they are here to stay. Like the traditions of my ancestors, the old-growth cultures of this land were ripped away and decomposed. Ours were borne in words and theirs in mycelium and scent. Both are lost to the ages now. 

We cannot bring them back but we can become something new. Like the trout. Almost anywhere they exist they are not "native." But a simple trout has blossomed into a hundred thousand different types, adapting to become distinct, adapting to their specific river. 

Perhaps if I stay my descendants will be indigenous.

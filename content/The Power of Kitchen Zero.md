(Because doing all your dishes and putting them away every day is basic responsibility, and [responsibility is power](responsibility%20is%20power.md))

In this new chapter in San Francisco, I've become a part of two cooperative organizations where the policy is what I call "kitchen zero". That means no dishes left in the sink, washed dried put away, sink and counter wiped, every time. 

At the daycare co-op this is extensive. Puddle Jumpers Workshop (PJW) keeps costs low by using the space of the Polish Club during the weekdays. Which means that all the toys -- the nap mats, the impressive collection of ride-on cars, the diaper bins and kids' tables and couch and books and play kitchen -- all of that needs to be put away perfectly out of sight, every night. Because there's swing dance in the same space later. 

The level of responsibility in PJW is immense. It's an organization comprised of parents who not only co-care for their children but also create the body of the self-perpetuating organization together. And it's beautiful to watch. Parents are just more present with their kids than a hired daycare worker -- the stakes are immeasurably higher. 

At the Embassy, a semi-famous coliving house that's been around for over a decacde, the policy is Kitchen Zero. While that's not always the reality, I've been adhering as best I can to the lofty goal of 'wash, dry, put away, every time'. (Plus, wiping the counters, etc). I thought it would be very difficult, as we cook all of our food at home and are also managing a toddler, but it's been easier to get into the habit than I expected. And I notice I feel better. Lighter, prouder -- I don't know. I have energy to spare. 

Maybe it's just the 'having childcare' talking, the having energy part. But I think there's something about this kitchen zero thing. 

As I am now dipping my toes into being a proselytizer of Kitchen Zero, I'm mulling over the eternal question: how do I talk to my roommate who's leaving lots of dirty dishes out? How do I get them to stop without being condescending and patronizing? 

What came up for me today was the question: why are you doing that?

What's your excuse? Why are you doing that? You are leaving dishes in the sink. Could you stop? 

If you are making messes and leaving dishes in the sink, you also spiritually have dishes in the sink. 

Try it out. Kitchen Zero. 

p.s. I do not claim to be a perfect adherent to Kitchen Zero.
p.p.s. If you try to put exceptions in the rules or get around Kitchen Zero, just a little mess here or there, then the bar is already lower and you are losing power. It's all about the high bar.

Social media vs social networking

Ami claims to be the first real social networking platform, which I think is a succinct description of how it's different from other social apps. 

Traditional Social Media companies, like Facebook/Meta (which includes Instagram), as well as advertising-based companies like Google, gather a lot of data about their users. But this data and the fruits that might come of its analysis is locked behind a walled garden. Data is very powerful for manipulating behavior, because the more you know about a person the more you can influence them. All of these traditional corporations take this data and manipulate their users for their own benefit: taking the user's attention as much as possible and serving them the largest amount of high-cost ads, to maximize advertising revenue. 

The word Media is inert, meaning a thing. Sure, social media is social -- it contains many social things -- but the goal of the company itself is just to have stuff that you'll look at to get your attention and keep it. 

This is different from the social networking concept. In Ami's model, wherein the business will sustain itself through subscription revenue, Ami must create enough utility for its users so that they will continue to pay. That means making it easier for people to make meaningful connections.
